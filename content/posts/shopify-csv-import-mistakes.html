A bulk import changes hundreds of live products in a few minutes. That's the point, and also the danger: one wrong column and every product page in a collection is broken before anyone notices. These are the mistakes we see most often, and how to avoid each one.

1. Blank cells that wipe your data

In Shopify's CSV import with "Overwrite products with matching handles" ticked, a blank cell in a column replaces the value in your store with nothing. Import a sheet with an empty Vendor column and every vendor is gone.

Avoid it: delete columns you don't mean to change from the file, rather than leaving them empty. Or use a tool that treats an empty cell as "leave it alone"; Bulk Product Uploader does, and only touches the columns you map.

2. Matching on a value that isn't unique

Two products called "Classic T-Shirt", or a SKU that was reused for a new colour, and the update lands on the wrong product, or on both.

Avoid it: match on SKU or barcode where you can, and check for duplicates in the sheet first (in Excel: select the column, Conditional Formatting, Highlight Cells Rules, Duplicate Values). Bulk Product Uploader only uses a match that finds exactly one product and reports the rest.

3. Variants that disappear

A sheet with only the sizes that changed can be read as "these are all the sizes", and the others get removed, along with their stock and sales history.

Avoid it: know what your tool does with variants that aren't in the file. In Bulk Product Uploader they're kept unless you untick "Keep existing variants that are not in the sheet".

4. Changed handles that break links

The handle is the product's web address. Change it and every link to the old address, from Google, ads, emails and social posts, leads to a missing page unless a redirect is set up.

Avoid it: don't change handles in a bulk update. Bulk Product Uploader only sets the handle of new products and never changes the address of an existing one.

5. Prices with text in them

"Rs. 1,299/-", "$19.99 USD" or "1.299,00" can be rejected, or read as a different number.

Avoid it: keep prices as plain numbers with a dot for decimals. Then check a few prices in the preview before uploading.

6. Publishing unfinished products

New products imported as active go live at once, with whatever text and images they have, even if the photos are still missing.

Avoid it: create new products as drafts, check them in the Shopify admin, then set them active. Draft is the default for new products in Bulk Product Uploader.

7. No way back

The worst mistake is the one you can't undo. Shopify's import has no undo button, so a bad file means fixing products by hand.

Avoid it: export your products before every big import. Better still, use a tool with backups: Bulk Product Uploader saves each product before changing it, and a restore puts back only what that upload changed.

A checklist for every bulk upload

  • Products exported or backed up
  • Only the columns you mean to change are included or mapped
  • Matching values (SKU, barcode) checked for duplicates
  • Prices are plain numbers
  • New products set to draft
  • Preview read, not just glanced at
  • A test run on five products, checked in Shopify, before the rest
